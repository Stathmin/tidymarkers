# Z8.1 — negative examples for training-set decomposition

Status: literature search, 2026-10-29. Agent scope: `data-raw/proposed/` only.
Companion machine-readable file: `z8.1-new-rows.csv`.

## What counts as a new row

A row is a **fully decomposed** negative example only when the paper prints, in the
text or in a numbered table/figure:

1. the **denominator** — how many assays were designed or tested, and
2. **every failed assay assigned to a stated cause**, and
3. the causes **sum to `n_tested − n_worked`** (the rule that makes
   `grewal2022_amblyopyrum` (10 + 251 + 49 + 31 + 22 = 363 = 698 − 335) and
   `makhoul2020_multi_assembly_sanger` (1 + 3 = 4 = 15 − 11) fully decomposed in
   `inst/extdata/conversion_rates.csv`).

A paper that prints only a success fraction is **denominator-only**. A paper whose
causes are stated with "mainly / mostly / several / some" is **qualitative** and is
never encoded as a count. A paper that reports only markers that worked is neither:
it has **no negative class**.

## Accepted — new fully decomposed rows

### 1. `yang2020_brassica_kasp` — Chinese cabbage, 268 assays (merged 2026-11-20)

Yang et al. 2020, _PLoS ONE_ 15(10):e0240042, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0240042`,
PMC7531813. "Development and validation of new KASP-SNP markers":

> "we selected 268 SNP loci to design markers from the above resource … In total, 258
> (96.27%) worked well and gave rise to polymorphisms, which categorized the four
> sequenced genotypes into two clusters, while the other ten primers produced no
> signal."

Source: PMC7531813 article body (Europe PMC `fullTextXML`).

Failure decomposition: **10 / 10 "produced no signal"** (no amplification). Sum
`10 = 268 − 258`. ✔ fully decomposed, single cause.

A second, independent classification is printed in the same paragraph and totals in
Table 3:

> "Among the 258 polymorphic markers, 221 (85.66%) were classified as co-dominant
> markers (Table 3) … The other 37 (14.34%) were classified as dominant markers,
> which means that one of the SNP genotypes produced a strong signal, while the
> signal of the other SNP genotype was considerably weaker (Table 3)."

Table 3 ("Codominant | Dominant | Total SNPs") sums per chromosome to 221 + 37 = 258,
so this is a **worked-set split and does not enter the failure decomposition**. It is
recorded in the CSV row (`n_worked_co_dominant=221`, `n_worked_dominant=37`) because a
dominant KASP marker cannot call heterozygotes — it is a resolution limit of the worked
set, not a failed assay.

Cause breakdown in the existing vocabulary: `n_no_amplification=10`.

### 2. `mariyamma2020_maize_kasp` — maize, 100 assays (compound class)

Jagtap et al. 2020, _Int J Mol Sci_ 21(19):7386,
DOI `10.3390/ijms21197386`, PMC7582619. Results §2.3 (KASP validation), with Figure 5:

> "All 100 MKAMs were validated on the parental lines LM11 and CML25 … **71 (71%) of
> these MKAMs were found to be polymorphic, while 21 (21%) were monomorphic and/or
> heterozygous type and were deemed unusable as markers. The remaining 8 (8%) failed
> to generate a useful amplification signal and thus were unusable (Figure 5).**"

Discussion restates it: "A total of 21% KASPs were generated only one or heterozygous
type genome call and were deemed unusable as markers, and 8% failed to generate a
useful amplification signal (unusable type)."

Sum `21 + 8 = 29 = 100 − 71`. ✔ closes.

**Caveat that must travel with this row — do not read it as `n_monomorphic=21`.** The
21 is a **compound printed class**: the paper writes "monomorphic **and/or**
heterozygous type" and never splits it, so an unknown share of the 21 is a
heterozygote-indistinguishable assay, not a monomorphic locus. In
`z8.1-new-rows.csv` the compound class is placed in `n_monomorphic` **only because
that is the first-listed cause in the paper's own label**; the `claim` cell carries
the qualification `COMPOUND … must not be split`. Treat this row as
`fully_decomposed_compound`, not as a clean monomorphic count. A second trap: the assay
was validated on the **two inbred parents only**, so every working assay gives a
single homozygous call by construction; "single-call" is vacuous here and the paper's
usable rule is polymorphism **between the parents**.

(An earlier draft of this document cited the paper as "Krishna et al." and the rice
paper as "Rodríguez et al." — both wrong. Correct authors: Jagtap et al. 2020 (maize)
and Steele et al. 2025 (rice), read from the article XML.)

## Accepted with reservation — printed decomposition that does not close

### `steele2025_rice_kasp` — rice, 4,000 assays (failure class not attributed)

Steele et al. 2025, _G3_ 15:jkae251, DOI `10.1093/g3journal/jkae251`,
PMC11708223. (Table 2 of this paper is also the only found instance of a
_predictor-property vs outcome_ table for failing KASP designs — see the Z8.2 note.)
Results, "Selection of 4,000 KASP for validation test …", Figure Aa of Supplementary
File S1 (figure; numbers also in the Results text):

> "Of the 4,000 trait-specific KASP designs tested, 3,371 were passed for wet-lab
> validation according to the service provider. But more stringent data analysis
> revealed that 3,366 KASP gave successful genotype calls in >90% of samples … hence,
> 3,366 were considered as validated. Eleven markers were monomorphic in this set of
> rice germplasm so data for the remaining 3,355 markers were used in cluster analysis"
>
> "Some 635 designs (15.9%) failed in all samples (Figure Aa in Supplementary File S1)
> and for designs with genotype calls below 90%, the lowest call rate was 17.4%."

- `n_tested=4,000`, `n_worked=3,366` (calls in >90% of samples), `n_failed=634`.
- `n_monomorphic=11` — but these 11 are **inside** the 3,366 worked set (they called
  genotypes in >90% of samples and were dropped only as uninformative in this panel),
  so they are **not** part of the 634 failures.
- The 634 itself is decomposed in two steps that do not close: 3,400 − 3,371 = 29
  assays that "failed" per the service provider but produced calls under the paper's
  stricter criterion, and 3,400 − 3,366 = 34 more. The paper does **not** split the 635
  "failed in all samples" figure by cause; it only notes in the Discussion that
  "Failures might be explained by the extracted DNA quality, the assay conditions in a
  particular genotyping run or they could potentially be due to issues related to using
  ambiguous bases in the designs."

Verdict: **not fully decomposed**. This is the strongest denominator-only row found —
it is a large, published denominator with a printed failure count, and the failure
class is explicitly unknown. Encode as denominator-only, not as a cause count.

## Why the existing three rows stay the only "clean" ones

The literature rarely prints, in the same sentence, (a) an assay denominator, (b) the
worked count, and (c) an exclusive split of the remainder. What is printed instead is
one of:

- a compound failure class ("monomorphic and/or heterozygous type") — maize row,
- an assay stage where the failing count is stated but its cause is left to the
  Discussion ("might be explained by DNA quality, assay conditions, ambiguous bases") —
  rice row,
- a filter on the _worked_ set (monomorphic across a small germplasm panel) reported
  as if it were a failure — the trap in the 268/258 cabbage row and the 554/286 noug
  row.

Both new rows are single-crop and single-cause-heavy: the cabbage row adds a pure
no-amplification class of 10, the maize row adds a _compound_ monomorphic/heterozygous
class of 21. Neither adds a clean "poor clustering" class, which remains absent from
every source found so far. If a second independent clean row is needed, the search
should target papers that print an assay-by-assay table (one row per marker, one
verdict column) — no such table was found in this sweep.

### What the two new rows do NOT support

- They do not support a crop-specific failure rate for soybean.
- They do not separate "monomorphic between the parents" from "het-indistinguishable";
  the maize row explicitly bundles them.
- They are all "work" scope (assay produces calls usable in the tested panel), never
  "diagnostic" scope.
