# Proposal: per-family mismatch-discrimination magnitudes (ROADMAP Z5.2)

Status: **proposed 2026-10-29.** Nothing in `R/` changes. `specificity_policy()`
keeps `calibrated = FALSE` for every scheme, and the CRISPR seed keeps `unknown`.

## The problem

`specificity_policy()` (`R/marker_specificity.R`, ~L1281) carries four rows and
`SPECIFICITY_SCHEME_ALIASES` folds every chemistry onto one of them: KASP, ARMS,
CAPS and HRM all become `pcr`; LAMP becomes `rpa`. One row therefore encodes
tolerances (`max_mismatch`, `seed_max`, `three_prime_max`, the position ladder)
that the literature measures on axes that are not comparable across families.
Z5.2 asked for the measured magnitude behind each row, so the policy can say
*why* a number is what it is - or state that no number exists.

## What the table holds

`inst/extdata/mismatch_family_magnitude.csv`, emitted by
`data-raw/mismatch_family_magnitude.R` (46 rows, one per family x condition x
axis end). It reads only extracts already in `inst/extdata/`; it re-scrapes
nothing. The generator asserts each source's own arithmetic (`stopifnot`), so a
changed input file fails loudly instead of silently moving a number.

## Per family: is the magnitude PREDICTIVE enough to drive the policy?

**RPA - measured, predicts direction and rank, NOT a per-target ratio.**
The 2022 study is the strongest source in the set: 4008 reactions, 315 mismatch
combinations, a positional model at AUC 0.88 narrowly beating a Gibbs-free-energy
model at 0.734. Its magnitude is a probability of a *binary* success call - a
perfect match keeps >= 0.984, the worst terminal pair keeps 0.59. That is a
penalty on the chance the reaction fires at all, not an efficiency ratio, so no
`weight_*` can be fitted to it. The practical consequence the policy already
encodes survives: **one terminal mismatch does not stop RPA**, and complete
inhibition needs an adjacent penultimate + terminal combination. The previous
conclusion (`rpa_anchor_rule.decision.md`: 4-nt anchor; offsets 1/2/3 not
ordered) is unchanged - this paper orders substitutions, not the 3' offsets.

**LAMP - measured, and it does not transfer; the RPA row is not obviously right.**
Two independent AS-LAMP assays show the same shape: with the mismatch on the
inner primers the unmatched allele does not amplify inside a 75-min run. That is
binary discrimination - the strongest outcome in the table - while the Tt delay
axis runs from -1.2 to 22.6 min depending on which primer end carries the
mismatch. LAMP is therefore **not** the same physics as RPA (where the terminal
probability bottoms out at 0.59, never 0), and the aliasing of `lamp` onto the
`rpa` row is a documented simplification, not a measured equivalence. Splitting
the row needs a decision that this task is not authorised to make.

**KASP / ARMS - measured, not a guaranteed gate.**
The ddCq spread (0.21 - 4.33, only 3 of 6 SNPs at 2 cycles) is wider than any
single tolerance number, and the classical yield classes (~100-fold severe,
~20-fold A:A) are context-dependent by the source's own caveat. Both families
inherit the `pcr` row, whose `three_prime_max = 0` is correct as a *position*
rule: the 3' terminus is where extension starts. The magnitude says the same
position is not reliably discriminating in practice, so the policy should
continue to reject on position, not on a predicted ddCq.

**CAPS - no magnitude exists.**
A restriction digest is binary; the only observable is whether the gel call
matches the in-silico digest. The verification panel is 6 of 10 markers clean,
which is a QC number, not a mismatch tolerance.

**HRM - measured, and resolution-limited rather than sequence-limited.**
dTm ranges 0.02 - 0.62 C for small amplicons, 0.4 - 0.7 C for single C>T SNPs in
one amplicon, and 0.20 - 0.69 C by bimorphism class with a free-energy index that
does not order the classes. The predictable part is that dTm is small and
locus-specific; the discriminating question is the instrument's resolution, not a
sequence rule. Already handled by `hrm_resolvability.decision.md`.

**CRISPR - `unknown`, and it stays `unknown`.**
The 2024 Cas12a screen measured ratios (0.767 - 1.394 on the unmodified crRNA,
0.227 - 1.199 on the optimised M39 crRNA, 20 positions each) and one pair reaches
0.227 - yet the ratios do not order by PAM distance, so no model predicts the
ratio for a new target. `R/marker_crispr.R` already carries this as
`crispr_seed_evidence()$magnitude = "unknown"`. **Not touched.**

## What changes

Nothing in `R/`. No policy number moves to `calibrated = TRUE`, because no family
produced a per-target predictive magnitude: RPA, LAMP, KASP and ARMS each give a
distribution that depends on the substitution and the context, CAPS has no axis,
and HRM's axis belongs to the instrument. The one reproducible cross-family fact
in the table is that a **lone 3'-terminal mismatch is not a gate anywhere** -
0.59 success in RPA, 3 of 6 SNPs at 2 cycles in KASP/ARMS - which is the
assertion the `three_prime_max` rows already make on position grounds.

## Open items this proposal deliberately leaves open

* Splitting the `lamp` alias off the `rpa` row (needs its own decision; the
  Magnitude table now supplies the evidence for it).
* A per-position RPA magnitude: Fig. 2/4 of the 2022 paper are heatmaps and print
  no per-position table, so only the substitution-level numbers are landed.
* KASP/ARMS on a second ddCq dataset: one study of 6 SNPs is a single sample of
  the spread.
