# Z5.2 — per-family mismatch magnitudes: what the literature now measures

Agent A2, 2026-10-29. Sources read through Europe PMC full text (list and
provenance in `a2.sources.csv`, extract ledger in `a2.ledger.csv`).

## What Z5.2 needed

`specificity_policy()` (`R/marker_specificity.R:1386`) returns one mismatch
policy for every scheme: `max_mismatch`, a seed window, and a 3′ window. The
blocker was never the *position* rule — `Z5.1` already scores a 3′-terminal
mismatch 12 and a middle mismatch 1. The blocker is that no family had a
*measured magnitude* to justify giving RPA, CRISPR and KASP different numbers,
and `crispr_seed_rule` measured a quantity without predictability
(`crispr_seed_rule.decision.md`).

Decision rule applied here: a family gets a split **only if** its literature
gives a number whose *shape* (which axis moves it) differs from the other
families, and that shape is measured in more than one assay geometry.

## Verdict per family

| family | predictive magnitude now exists? | shape | enough to split `specificity_policy()`? |
|---|---|---|---|
| RPA | **no** (unchanged) | binary reaction success, not a magnitude | **no** |
| LAMP | **partial** | tolerance by primer end (Tt delay) **and** endpoint melting separation | **no** (one family, two readouts, same single-source Tt axis) |
| CRISPR | **yes, but not as a transferable fold-signal** | mismatch **number** (2 vs 3 in a 23-nt spacer) × spacer length × target load; a *probability* that anything moves, not a per-target ratio | **yes for a number rule, no for a magnitude** |
| KASP / ARMS | **yes** | 3′-anchor **position** and a second mismatch at −3; call rate 98 % | **no** — the existing 3′ penalty already encodes it |
| CAPS / HRM | **yes (HRM), no (CAPS)** | ΔTm per substitution, 0.4–1.7 °C, positionally scattered | **no** for HRM (already a ΔTm gate), **no data** for CAPS |

## RPA — no magnitude

The package already holds the RPA magnitude that exists:
`rpa_mismatch_terminal.csv` (Higgins 2022) gives the *probability a reaction
still fires* per terminal mismatch, 0.59 (C–C) to 0.984, and
`mismatch_family_magnitude.csv` classifies it as binary success, not an
efficiency ratio. I searched for the missing axis — time-to-threshold as a
function of mismatch *number* — and did not find a usable number: the Europe
PMC hits for "recombinase polymerase amplification" × "number of mismatches"
are application papers (eDNA, virus panels) quoting RPA tolerance, not
measuring it; the one dedicated SNP paper (`10.1016/j.ab.2025.115919`) has no
open full text in the REST API and no PMC id.

**Consequence.** RPA keeps a single number (`max_mismatch`) because no paper
prints how much slower a 2- or 3-mismatch primer is. This is the gap the
`Z5.2` note originally named ("RPA, several mismatches") and it is still open.

## LAMP — tolerance yes, discrimination only by melting

Two different measurements, both already in the package:

1. **Tt delay by primer end** (`lamp_mismatch_rtlamp2019_position_rules.csv`,
   Front Microbiol 2019): F3 1.1–3.5 min, F1c 6.9–13.4, F2 13.5–17.3, BLP
   17.3–22.6 min, and −1.2 min under the novel polymerase. The sign runs both
   ways, so this is a tolerance magnitude, not a discriminating one.
2. **Endpoint melting separation** (new, `mismatch_family_lamp.csv`,
   Microbiol Spectr 2025, PMC12671098): SS14 92.46 ± 0.13 °C vs Nichols
   93.71 ± 0.21 °C, 1.25 °C against a stated ± 1 °C margin.

The melting row says something the policy does not use: a LAMP allele call can
live in the *melting peak*, not in a Tt window. That is a **readout** choice
and belongs to the readout layer, not to the mismatch policy. The primer-site
mismatch itself is still unquantified in every source I read.

**Consequence.** No split. A LAMP policy would only differ from the PCR policy
by the size of the Tt penalty, and that penalty is one paper on one target
system.

## CRISPR — the number exists, a magnitude does not

This is the substantive change. Five sources, read in full:

| source | what it measures | number |
|---|---|---|
| Sci Adv 2024, PMC11654686 (RfxCas13d, 23-nt spacer) | mismatch **number** | 2 MM with SNP → 54.8 % silencing; 3 MM with WT → 11.0 % residual; 3 MM is "exceeding the upper limit" |
| NAR 2024, PMC10810210 (LbuCas13a) | spacer **length** × target load | 28-nt spacer: no single-MM discrimination; 20-nt: up to 70 % activity loss (middle), 40 % SNR loss at +7, ≤ 20 % at +4/+11; 16-nt: perfect match required |
| Chem Sci 2021, PMC8179484 (Cas12a) | a designed 2nd mismatch | 2-fold → nearly 10-fold signal ratio |
| Nat Commun 2026, PMC13473493 (Cas12a, one-pot) | PAM triplet | 2.8–6.0-fold (TTT) vs 5.9–18.4-fold (ATT) SNP/WT |
| Nat Biotechnol 2026, PMC12584110 (Cas13) | inertness without an occluder | 74/96 mismatches lower activity at all; 13/24 significant; 24/24 with an occluder; ~50-fold ratio gain |

The shape is a **gate, not a gradient**: below a mismatch count the effector
fires at nearly full strength (a single mismatch usually leaves Cas13 at or
near perfect-match activity, `communbiol2024_crrna_ratio.csv` ratios
0.77–1.39 confirm this from the other direction), and above it the reaction
collapses. Where the gate sits is not fixed — 2–3 MM at 23 nt, none usable at
28 nt, unusable below 20 nt — and the Cas12a allele *contrast* is a 2.8–18.4×
band whose position inside the band is set by the PAM and by sequence context
the authors themselves call unpredictable.

**What can be encoded now** is therefore a *count rule with a length gate*:
`n_mismatches >= 3` worth `unknown`/hard-miss at spacer length ≈ 23 nt,
`specificity_policy()`'s current `seed_len = 8, seed_max = 1` unchanged for
Cas12a (its seed-region sensitivity is real, `crispr_seed_rule.decision.md`),
and no per-target fold-signal anywhere. Whether that splits the policy is a
`R/` decision and out of this agent's write scope; the recommendation is
**split the CRISPR branch on mismatch count and spacer length, keep one
magnitude-free gate**.

**Unchanged:** `crispr_seed_rule` stays `unknown`. None of these papers
predict a per-target ratio from sequence.

## KASP / ARMS — the 3′-anchor is already encoded

Lefever 2019 (`inst/extdata/lefever_single_mismatch.csv`) gives the
per-SNP ddCq (min 0.21 → max 4.33; 3/6 separate at 2 cycles) and the new
`mismatch_family_arms.csv` adds the two facts the policy was missing: a single
3′-terminal mismatch "is not sufficient for reliable genotype calling", and a
second mismatch at position −3 lifts correct calls to 376/384 = 98 % at a
5-dCq cut-off. The Kwok yield classes (~100× severe, ~20× A:A) were already
in `mismatch_discrimination.csv`.

**Consequence.** No split needed: this *is* the 3′ anchor the policy already
models. The only new number worth carrying is the −3 optimum, which is a
design-time recommendation, not a scoring weight.

## CAPS / HRM

HRM has a per-count magnitude from an extract the package already holds
(`hrm_ijms2022_dtm_single_snp_controls.csv`): 0 T alleles 0.0 °C, one 0.4 /
0.7 / 0.6 °C, two 1.0 / 1.3 °C, three 1.7 °C. `mismatch_family_hrm.csv` restates
it as 0.57 °C per substitution with a 0.3 °C locus spread — i.e. **the
magnitude is real but non-transferable between loci**, which is exactly why HRM
is already gated on ΔTm rather than on a mismatch count. CAPS: no magnitude
paper found; the family coverage sheet already records it as a verification
funnel, not a discrimination model.

## Still `unknown` (explicit list)

1. **RPA multi-mismatch number curve.** No paper read gives signal, Tt or
   success rate as a function of 1/2/3 mismatches under real RPA conditions.
   `rpa_mismatch_terminal.csv` covers ONE terminal mismatch only.
2. **CRISPR per-target fold-signal.** Five papers agree the magnitude is not
   predictable from sequence; the Cas12a contrast band (2.8–18.4×) is set
   empirically.
3. **CRISPR spacer length × concentration joint law.** The gate moves with
   both (NAR 2024) but no functional form is printed.
4. **Cas13d in a diagnostic readout.** Sci Adv 2024 measures silencing in
   cells; the threshold may not survive conversion to fluorescence or
   lateral flow. NAR 2026 and Nat Biotechnol 2026 show the diagnostic versions
   behave differently.
5. **LAMP primer-site ΔTt for a *specific* mismatch type.** One source, one
   target system.
6. **HRM per-substitution ΔTm transferability.** 0.3 °C spread between three
   identical substitutions in one amplicon; no source maps it to nearest-
   neighbour thermodynamics for this use.
7. **KASP across platforms.** The 98 % call rate is one platform (LightCycler
   480) and synthetic templates.
